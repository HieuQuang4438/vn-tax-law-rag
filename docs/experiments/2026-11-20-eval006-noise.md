# Run-to-run noise in EVAL-006

Of the run-to-run change in EVAL-006, how much comes from the answer model and how much from the
judge, and how many answers per question bring the paired 95% interval of an EVAL-006 change to
±0.03 or narrower on eval set v2? The question is #37; the approach is the approved comment on
#37, steps 0A, 1A, 2A and 3B, and the later approved comment *a seed per run*. The tools are
`docs/reference/eval-results.md`, *Comparing several runs*, *Re-judging a run* and *Run-to-run
noise*, and the seed in `docs/reference/answer.md`, *Seed*.

#34's prompt moved EVAL-006 by +0.027 [−0.016, +0.071] against `69f6ab4` and was recorded as
inconclusive ([2026-11-19-every-part-prompt.md](2026-11-19-every-part-prompt.md)). Two runs of one
answer configuration had already changed the verdict of 11 of 184 items
([2026-11-19-point-level-ids.md](2026-11-19-point-level-ids.md)).

**Configuration.** Everything as at `69f6ab4` (`eval/results/2026-11-19-69f6ab4.json`): eval set
v2 (`f14d299e`), the same index (bge-m3 `5617a9f6`, `syllable` BM25, 1,286 chunks), `TOP_K` 8,
`RRF_K` 60, 50 candidates, one hit per article, `rewrite: none`, answer and judge
`gemini-3.5-flash-lite`, the same answer and judge prompts (by hash), budget 24,000. The code of
generation and judging is that of `69f6ab4`; the commits after it add the tools of this issue and
the seed. Dev laptop (i7-10610U, 16 GB RAM, CPU only), Gemini API.

**Decision rule** (in #37, fixed before the runs). k is the smallest number of answers per question
whose projected half-width of a paired EVAL-006 change is ±0.03 or narrower. A majority-vote judge
is proposed as its own issue when the re-judge changes 6 or more of the 184 verdicts.

The bootstrap check agrees with k = 1: the three pairs above give ±0.035 to ±0.043, around the
projected ±0.040.

## Conclusion

- **k = 2.** It is the smallest number of answers per question whose projected half-width, ±0.028,
  is within ±0.03. A generation experiment runs each arm twice, with `--seed 1` and `--seed 2`, and
  compares them with `python -m src.eval.compare` (`docs/reference/eval-results.md`, *Run-to-run
  noise*). The k is a floor: a real change that helps some items and hurts others widens the
  interval beyond the projection.
- **The answer model is most of the noise.** Between two runs, 75 to 92 of 184 answers keep their
  text, and the judge alone changes 4 verdicts against 12 to 17.
- Three runs of one configuration gave EVAL-006 from 0.348 to 0.386. The 0.386 of `69f6ab4` is the
  highest of the three.

**Not a decision, an observation for the rerun of #34's prompt.** `python -m src.eval.compare --old`
the three runs `--new eval/results/2026-11-19-0dd6f44.json` gives EVAL-006 0.364 → 0.413, +0.049
[+0.016, +0.083]. It was looked at after the fact, its new arm is one unseeded run, and the old
arm's mean includes the two runs this issue added, so it decides nothing. It is what a rerun of the
prompt with k = 2 per arm should test.

The generation rows of *Standing scores* now hold the mean of the three runs (EVAL-006 0.364,
0.299–0.431), as the register's rule for several runs of one configuration says.

Judged scores are not hand-checked. In all, about 4.7 USD: the re-judge 0.76, the probe's 10 calls
about 0.05, the two runs 3.90.
